5 Things to Know About Rattlesnakes and Their Babies

UNLV College of Pharmacy | Aug. 6, 2014

Warning!

Arguably, snake season is year-round in Nevada, a state known for its rattlers. But baby rattlesnakes are born in July and August, making these two months especially dangerous for hikers, gardeners, children and others at high risk of exposure to rattlesnake bites.

Reports

So far this year, 35 rattlesnake bites to humans have been reported to the UNLV Poison and Drug Information Center. Based at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas College of Pharmacy, the center serves the entire state of Nevada with the exception of Nye County, providing free and confidential poison and medication information to callers around the clock.

Pharmacy Hotline

Specialists answering the phones at the center regularly receive calls from Las Vegans of all ages who don't realize they were bitten by a rattler. The poison center urges anyone who feels a mysterious sting, pinch or bite while outdoors to immediately call the center at 800-702-7777.

"We will ask a few questions that will help you either identify possible snakebite or eliminate it," said Keith Boesen, director of the Las Vegas Poison and Drug Information Center. "With snakebite, the sooner the medical treatment, the better the outcome, so calling us right away can make a very big difference for the victims and the medical teams treating them."

Stay alert, Stay safe

The center advises anyone who might come cross paths with rattlesnakes to be aware of these five things:

  2. Baby rattlesnakes are rattleless until they first shed their skins...
  3. Despite their impish size, baby snakes have enough venom...
  4. Adult rattlesnakes do not always rattle an audible warning...
  5. It's a good idea to call the poison center...
